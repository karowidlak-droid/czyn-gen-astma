# Comprehensive Expert Review: Clinical Pulmonology, Genetics, and Statistics

**Manuscript Title:** Genetic Factors Influencing Asthma Exacerbation Frequency: A Systematic Review of Candidate Gene Polymorphisms

As an expert panel encompassing clinical pulmonology, statistical genetics, and meta-analytic methodology, we have reviewed the revised manuscript. The authors have commendably addressed previous feedback regarding the 17q21 Linkage Disequilibrium block, clinical endotyping (T2 vs. non-T2), the inclusion of *FCER2*/*GLCCI1*, and the statistical limitations of underpowered meta-analyses ($k=3$) and candidate gene "winner's curses."

However, to meet the highest standards for publication in top-tier respiratory journals (e.g., *The Journal of Asthma* or *BMC Pulmonary Medicine*), the following nuanced issues must be addressed:

## 1. Ambiguity in the Phenotypic Definition of "Exacerbation"
From a clinical and statistical standpoint, the manuscript treats "asthma exacerbations" somewhat monolithically in the meta-analysis and general text, despite acknowledging endotypes later.
* **The Issue:** The statistical definition of an exacerbation (e.g., OCS prescription vs. Emergency Department visit vs. hospitalization) drastically alters the genetic signal. A variant associated with an ED visit may simply reflect poor healthcare access or medication adherence, whereas a variant associated with ICU admission reflects profound biological vulnerability.
* **Recommendation:** The manuscript must explicitly state the phenotypic definitions used in the primary studies for key variants (e.g., defining severe AEs as requiring $\ge3$ days of systemic corticosteroids) and critically evaluate how phenotypic heterogeneity statistically confounds cross-study comparisons.

## 2. Inadequate Discussion of Eurocentric Bias in Polygenic Risk Scores (PRS)
In the "Conclusions" and "Future Research Directions" sections, the authors advocate for integrating Polygenic Risk Scores (PRS) into personalized pulmonology.
* **The Issue:** A major statistical and genetic limitation of current PRS models is their overwhelming derivation from European-ancestry populations (Eurocentric bias). Applying these scores to admixed or non-European populations often leads to severe statistical attenuation (reduced predictive power) and exacerbates existing healthcare disparities.
* **Recommendation:** Explicitly caution that before PRS can be utilized for "preemptive genotypic stratification," the models must be validated and recalibrated in diverse, multi-ancestry cohorts (e.g., African American and Hispanic populations, where asthma morbidity is disproportionately high).

## 3. Epigenetic and Pharmaco-epigenetic Blind Spots
While the review covers SNPs and eQTLs, asthma exacerbation is fundamentally a disease of Gene $\times$ Environment interactions.
* **The Issue:** The review focuses solely on static germline DNA variants. However, acute severe exacerbations (especially those driven by smoking, pollution, or viral infections) are heavily mediated by dynamic epigenetic modifications (e.g., DNA methylation at the *SMAD3* or *IL4* loci).
* **Recommendation:** The "Biological Interpretations" or "Limitations" section should briefly acknowledge that static candidate gene SNPs represent only a fraction of the regulatory landscape, and future multi-omics approaches must integrate epigenetic data to capture the full gene-environment interaction triggering acute flares.

## 4. Disconnect Between GRADE Quality and Clinical Recommendations
The authors provide an excellent GRADE summary table (Table S4).
* **The Issue:** Variants such as *TGFB1* rs1800469 and *SEMA3D* rs993312 are rated as having "Very Low" to "Low" overall quality due to serious risk of bias, inconsistency, and imprecision. Yet, in the main text, they are still discussed alongside "High" quality loci like *GSDMB* without sufficient statistical caveats regarding their low confidence.
* **Recommendation:** The narrative synthesis in the main text should directly reference the GRADE quality assessments. For example, explicitly state that the evidence supporting *TGFB1* and *SEMA3D* is currently of "Very Low" quality and therefore cannot be used to inform clinical pathways or predictive models at this time.
