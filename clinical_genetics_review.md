# Clinical Pulmonology and Genetics Review Report

**Manuscript Title:** Genetic Factors Influencing Asthma Exacerbation Frequency: A Systematic Review of Candidate Gene Polymorphisms

As an expert in pulmonology and respiratory genetics, I have reviewed the manuscript. While the authors present a comprehensive synthesis of candidate gene associations, the clinical and biological interpretations require refinement to align with current mechanistic understanding and precision medicine paradigms. Below are the key issues identified:

## 1. Oversimplification of the 17q12-21.2 Locus (GSDMB/ORMDL3)
The manuscript discusses *GSDMB* and *ORMDL3* variants (rs7216389, rs4794820) as if they are distinct and independent genetic signals. In reality, the 17q12-21.2 locus is characterized by extreme Linkage Disequilibrium (LD) in European populations. Variants in this region are co-inherited as a massive haplotype block. Ascribing specific functional causality to *GSDMB* versus *ORMDL3* based purely on GWAS or candidate SNP associations is genetically inaccurate without deep functional validation (e.g., CRISPR editing or organoid models). The review must explicitly state that these SNPs are proxy markers for the entire 17q21 haplotype block.

## 2. Lack of Clinical Endotyping (T2-High vs. T2-Low Exacerbations)
Asthma exacerbations are highly heterogeneous. A major limitation of this review is the failure to systematically stratify genetic associations by exacerbation endotype.
* *IL4RA* and *IL10* variants heavily drive Type-2 (Eosinophilic) exacerbations.
* *TGFB1* and *CHI3L1* variants are more closely aligned with structural airway remodeling and non-T2 (Neutrophilic or paucigranulocytic) exacerbations.
Lumping all exacerbations together dilutes the clinical utility of the findings. The manuscript should attempt to map these genes to specific inflammatory endotypes where the source data permits.

## 3. Omission of Crucial Pharmacogenes (*FCER2*, *GLCCI1*, *CRHR1*)
The manuscript includes *ADRB2* and *NR3C1* but omits highly validated pharmacogenes that dictate exacerbation frequency under treatment pressure.
* *FCER2* (rs28364072) is a well-established driver of severe exacerbations in children utilizing Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS).
* *GLCCI1* and *CRHR1* strongly predict ICS treatment failure and subsequent loss of asthma control.
Omitting these genes in a review focused on exacerbation frequency (which is inherently tied to treatment failure) creates a significant gap in the literature synthesis.

## 4. Conflating eQTLs with Direct Pathophysiologic Mechanisms
In sections discussing *GSDMB* and *CTNNA3*, the authors cite expression Quantitative Trait Loci (eQTL) data to support biological mechanisms (e.g., "An eQTL upregulating GSDMB and ORMDL3"). The authors must clarify that eQTLs prove a statistical association with mRNA abundance in a specific tissue at a specific time, not a definitive pathophysiologic mechanism. Correlation does not equal causation in functional genomics.

## 5. Epistasis and Gene-Gene Interactions
The manuscript briefly mentions a "powerful interaction" between *GSDMB* and *CDHR3* but largely treats genetic risk additively. Exacerbations triggered by rhinovirus (RV-C) rely on *CDHR3* as a viral entry receptor, which operates upstream of *GSDMB*-mediated pyroptosis. The review should expand on epistasis (gene-gene interactions) to better explain the biological pathways of virus-induced exacerbations.

## Recommendations for Revision:
1. **Consolidate the 17q21 Locus:** Revise the *GSDMB* and *ORMDL3* sections to acknowledge the LD block and treat the locus holistically.
2. **Incorporate Endotyping:** Add a brief section or table column attempting to classify these genetic variants by their corresponding exacerbation phenotype (T2 vs. non-T2).
3. **Include Key Pharmacogenes:** Add *FCER2* and *GLCCI1* to the systematic search and results, or explicitly justify their exclusion in the methodology.
4. **Refine Mechanistic Language:** Soften definitive mechanistic claims derived solely from associative eQTL data.
