# Peer Review Report

**Manuscript Title:** Genetic Factors Influencing Asthma Exacerbation Frequency: A Systematic Review of Candidate Gene Polymorphisms
**Target Journal:** Journal of Asthma / BMC Pulmonary Medicine
**Reviewer Recommendation:** Major Revision

## General Comments
The authors present a systematic review aiming to synthesize the evidence linking specific candidate gene polymorphisms to the frequency and severity of asthma exacerbations. The topic is highly relevant to the field of precision pulmonology. The manuscript is well-written, logically structured, and successfully attempts to integrate clinical phenotypes with molecular mechanisms, such as airway remodeling and type 2 inflammation.

However, from a methodological and statistical standpoint, there are several significant discrepancies between the promised methods and the reported results. The manuscript currently falls short of the rigorous standards expected for a PRISMA-compliant systematic review and meta-analysis. Major revisions are required to ensure data transparency, statistical rigor, and methodological reproducibility.

## Major Issues

### 1. Incomplete PRISMA Reporting and Search Strategy
*   **Search Strategy (Table S1):** The search strings provided in Supplementary Table S1 are overly simplified. A reproducible search strategy must include exact syntax, medical subject headings (MeSH terms/Emtree), boolean operators, and the exact date the search was executed. Furthermore, the search limits (e.g., language restrictions, human subjects) must be specified.
*   **PRISMA Diagram (Figure 1):** The flow diagram claims 89 studies were included in the final synthesis. However, Supplementary Table S2 ("Characteristics of Included Studies") only lists 7 studies/cohorts. A systematic review must provide a complete extraction table containing the baseline characteristics of *all* 89 included studies.

### 2. Deficient Meta-Analytical Reporting
*   **Missing Statistical Metrics:** In Section 2.8, the authors promise to evaluate heterogeneity (using $I^2$ and Cochran's Q) and publication bias (Funnel plots, Egger's test). However, these metrics are completely absent from the Results section and the meta-analysis presented for *ADRB2* rs1042713.
*   **Forest Plot Deficiencies (Figure 3):** The forest plot provided for *ADRB2* rs1042713 is highly schematic and inadequate for publication. A proper forest plot must display the exact Odds Ratio (OR), 95\% Confidence Intervals (CI), and the statistical weight assigned to each individual study (BREATHE, PACMAN, GALA II), alongside the pooled summary estimate and heterogeneity statistics ($I^2$, $p$-value).
*   **Lack of Meta-Analysis for Other Loci:** The authors state that meta-analyses were performed where $\ge 3$ comparable studies were available. It is highly likely that loci such as *IL4* rs2243250 or *GSDMB* rs7216389 meet this criterion based on the narrative text, yet no pooled quantitative synthesis or forest plots are provided for them. If pooling was not possible due to clinical heterogeneity, this must be explicitly justified.

### 3. Superficial Risk of Bias and Evidence Quality (GRADE) Assessments
*   **Risk of Bias (Section 3.3 \& Figure 2):** The manuscript presents a generic summary of the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) scores (e.g., "75\% Low Risk"). For a systematic review, the authors must provide a detailed table (presumably intended for Table S3) listing the exact score for each included study across all NOS domains.
*   **GRADE Assessment:** The "SNP Ranking by Evidence" table in the Supplementary Materials states it is based on the GRADE framework. However, it only provides a final "High/Moderate/Low" rating. The authors must present a formal GRADE Summary of Findings (SoF) table that explicitly grades the evidence based on the five standard domains: Risk of Bias, Inconsistency, Indirectness, Imprecision, and Publication Bias.

### 4. Biological Interpretation and Nomenclature
*   **Genetic Nomenclature:** Throughout the text, the authors occasionally use non-standard formatting for nucleotide substitutions (e.g., `11377C>G` in Section 4.12). These should be properly formatted (e.g., $11377\text{C}>\text{G}$) to comply with HGVS sequence variant nomenclature guidelines.
*   **Mechanism vs. Association:** In several sections (e.g., *CTNNA3*), the text heavily relies on eQTL data and in vitro mechanistic findings to explain exacerbation risk. While highly valuable, the authors must clearly delineate which findings are derived directly from the clinical exacerbation cohorts versus which are hypothesized from secondary mechanistic databases.

## Minor Issues / Nitpicks
*   **Formatting:** Ensure all gene symbols (e.g., ADRB2, IL4RA) are consistently italicized throughout the manuscript when referring to the gene, and non-italicized when referring to the protein product.
*   **Figure References:** Double-check all cross-references to figures and tables. Ensure that placeholders like "(Available in online data repository)" are replaced with actual data or proper appendix citations prior to publication.

## Conclusion
This manuscript addresses an important topic but requires substantial methodological tightening. The authors must provide the raw data, exact statistical outputs for the meta-analysis, and complete characteristic tables for all included studies to warrant publication in a high-impact journal.
