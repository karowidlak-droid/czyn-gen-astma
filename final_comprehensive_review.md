# Final Comprehensive Expert Review: Pulmonology, Genetics, and Statistics

**Manuscript Title:** Genetic Factors Influencing Asthma Exacerbation Frequency: A Systematic Review of Candidate Gene Polymorphisms

As a multidisciplinary expert panel, we have reviewed the latest iteration of the manuscript. The authors have done an exemplary job addressing the vast majority of our previous concerns, successfully integrating complex discussions on endotyping, epistasis, the 17q21 LD block, statistical powering, and Eurocentric PRS biases.

The manuscript is now highly rigorous and robust. However, for a systematic review of this caliber, there are a few remaining, highly granular issues that, if addressed, would transition this manuscript from "excellent" to "definitive in the field."

## 1. The Missing Piece: Eosinophil Dynamics and *IL5*/*IL5RA*
While the authors comprehensively address the Type 2 (T2) inflammation pathway via *IL4*, *IL4RA*, and *IL10*, there is a glaring omission of the *IL5* and *IL5RA* axis.
* **The Clinical Issue:** In severe, exacerbation-prone asthma, eosinophilic flares are predominantly driven by IL-5 signaling. Biologics targeting this pathway (e.g., mepolizumab, benralizumab) are the cornerstone of severe asthma management.
* **Recommendation:** The genetic variants influencing baseline eosinophil counts and *IL5RA* expression must be explicitly acknowledged in the T2-High endotype section. Even if GWAS data for *IL5RA* is sparse regarding isolated exacerbation frequency, its biological indispensability to the T2-High phenotype requires a brief mention in the "Biological Interpretations" section to provide a complete clinical picture.

## 2. Statistical Treatment of GWAS Catalog Search Methodology
* **The Methodological Issue:** In Supplementary Table S1, the search strategy for the GWAS Catalog is listed merely as `(Asthma) AND (Exacerbation)`. From an informatics perspective, the GWAS Catalog relies on specific, standardized EFO (Experimental Factor Ontology) traits. Searching free-text terms in this database often yields high false-negative rates.
* **Recommendation:** To ensure scientific rigor and reproducibility, the manuscript should specify the actual EFO trait codes used to query the GWAS Catalog (e.g., EFO_0000270 for asthma, coupled with severe/exacerbation sub-traits), rather than presenting a free-text Boolean string.

## 3. Ambiguity in "Pediatric vs Adult Asthma" Transitions
* **The Genetic/Clinical Issue:** In section 5.4, the authors state: *"Pediatric exacerbations are heavily driven by epithelial barrier loci... In contrast, adult-onset asthma exacerbations involve chronic metabolic pathways."* While generally true, this creates a false dichotomy. Genetic risk operates on a life-course trajectory. Variants like *GSDMB* operate predominantly in childhood due to viral-naivety, but their impact wanes. Conversely, *ADIPOQ* risk increases with time due to the accumulated environmental exposure of obesity.
* **Recommendation:** Refine the language in Section 5.4 to describe this as a "Life-Course Genetic Trajectory" rather than a strict pediatric vs. adult dichotomy, acknowledging that the genetic architecture shifts dynamically over time as environmental exposures accumulate.

## Summary
Addressing these three final, nuanced points will cement the manuscript's status as a leading, flawless review in the field of respiratory genomics.
